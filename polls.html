How to read these numbers

  • A poll is a snapshot, not a prediction. Most have a margin of error of 3 to 5 points, so a lead smaller than that is close to a tie.
  • The big number is an average of the most recent polls from the last 30 days (up to six). With fewer polls, it uses the latest ones available.
  • (R) or (D) after a pollster's name means it works for or leans toward that party. Weigh those with care.
  • Ratings come from the Cook Political Report, Sabato's Crystal Ball and Inside Elections, independent analysts who rate how competitive each race is.
  • Where the numbers come from: the public polling tables for each race on Wikipedia, which volunteer editors update as polls are released and which link every poll to its source. Candidate names are fixed on our end. Polls sponsored by national mainstream outlets are left out.
  • queerham does not endorse candidates or parties. This page shows the numbers so you can make your own call.
